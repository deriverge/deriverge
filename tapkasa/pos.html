What it looks like at the stall

A customer asks for two mulled wines and a pastry. You tap mulled wine twice, the pastry once, and the amount is at the bottom of the screen. They hand you a twenty, you tap 20 and Tapkasa shows the change. It takes a few seconds and nobody does sums in their head, not even a helper on their first shift.

Two phones: till and pickup

When a queue builds up, one phone takes the money and the other shows the orders with numbers. The pickup phone ticks off what is ready and can read new orders aloud, so you do not have to look at a screen with your hands full. Two iPhones or two Android phones link directly with no internet; an iPhone and an Android phone need a connection.

Deposits on cups and mugs

The “+ Add deposit” button adds two items to your menu: Deposit and Deposit return. A returned cup is taken off the bill, and the totals show deposits taken and returned separately, so they never get mixed up with your sales.

Know your takings at the end of the day

The overview shows total sales, cash and card separately, and how many of each item you sold. You can save a sales summary as a PDF, for example for your bookkeeper.

How it works

  1. Type in your menu

    Each item gets a name, a price and a colour. It takes a few minutes at home, or start from the sample menu.

  2. Tap the cards

    A tap adds one, a long press takes one off. The amount due is always at the bottom.

  3. Cash or card

    For cash, Tapkasa works out the change. Take cards on your own reader and just mark the sale as card in Tapkasa.

What Tapkasa is not

  • It is not a certified or fiscal cash register and it reports nothing to tax authorities. The sales summary is not a tax invoice or a customer receipt.
  • It does not process card payments. You take cards on your own reader and Tapkasa only records the sale.
  • It does not print receipts or tickets.

For stalls, fairs and occasional selling that is usually all you need. Check with your accountant or tax office which rules apply to you.

What it costs

Tapkasa Pro: unlimited bills and linking the till with a pickup device. It is a monthly or yearly subscription through the App Store or Google Play, with a 14-day free trial for new subscribers. One subscription is enough for a stall: the second phone joins a device with Tapkasa Pro for free.

Questions people ask

Do I need an internet connection?

No. Selling, totals and backups all run on the phone. You only need internet to download the app, to buy Tapkasa Pro and to link an iPhone with an Android phone.

Can a helper or volunteer use it?

Yes. A sale is a tap on a coloured card and a choice of Cash or Card. An item added by mistake comes off with a long press.

Is Tapkasa free?

Yes, for up to 10 paid bills a day on one device. Unlimited bills and linking two devices need the Tapkasa Pro subscription, which new subscribers can try free for 14 days.

Is it a certified or fiscal cash register?

No. Tapkasa counts and records your sales, but it is not a certified register, sends nothing to tax authorities and does not issue tax invoices.

Where is my data?

Only on your device. Tapkasa has no accounts and no cloud. You can save a backup file and load it on a new phone.

Which languages and currencies does it support?

Nine languages (English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Polish, Czech and Slovak) and 33 currencies, each with its own notes for working out change.
