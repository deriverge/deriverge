Quote or estimate?

A quote is a fixed price for a defined job. An estimate is your best guess, and the final bill can turn out higher. Customers are told to ask for a quote for exactly that reason, so say clearly at the top which one this is. If you give a quote, describe the work precisely and say what happens when something unexpected turns up, because you cannot simply charge more later.

What to put in a builder's quote

  • your name or business name, address and contact details, and your VAT number if you are registered,
  • the customer's name and address, and the address of the job,
  • a quote number, the date and how long the price is valid,
  • a clear description of the work, with labour and materials broken down,
  • what is not included, for example making good, skips, scaffolding or building control fees,
  • the total price, and the VAT if you charge it,
  • the start date and how long the job should take,
  • payment terms: deposit, stage payments and when the balance is due,
  • a place for the customer to sign and date their acceptance.

Why the price, the dates and the scope belong in writing

Under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 you must carry out the work with reasonable care and skill. If no price was agreed, the customer only has to pay a reasonable price, and if no time was agreed, the work must be done within a reasonable time. A written quote with a price, a start date and a duration removes those arguments before they start. What you tell a customer about the job can also become part of the contract, so keep the description accurate.

VAT on building work

You must register for VAT when your taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in a rolling 12 month period. If you are not registered, do not show VAT at all. If you are, the standard rate is 20%. Some work has a reduced or zero rate: for example, the installation of certain energy saving materials in homes is zero rated until 31 March 2027. Check HMRC's guidance for the job before you rely on a lower rate.

The construction domestic reverse charge only applies between VAT registered businesses under CIS. It does not apply when you work for a homeowner.

Quotes agreed at the customer's home

If the contract is made at the customer's home or away from your business premises, a consumer usually has 14 days to cancel under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013. You must give them written information about this right. If you do not, the cancellation period can run for up to a year longer. If the customer wants you to start within the 14 days, get that request in writing, on paper or by e-mail. There are exceptions, for example for urgent repairs the customer asked you to come out for, so check which rules apply to your job.

Tips to win the job

  • Send the quote the same day as the survey.
  • Offer options, for example standard and premium tiles, so the customer chooses between your prices.
  • Add photos of the current state so the scope is clear.
  • Make it easy to say yes: a signature on the spot beats a printout on the fridge.

Questions people ask

Is a quote legally binding?

Once the customer accepts a quote, you have a contract at that price for the work described. That is why the description, the exclusions and how long the price stands matter.

How long should a quote be valid?

30 days is common. If material prices are moving quickly, choose a shorter period and say so on the quote.
