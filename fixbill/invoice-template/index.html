What to put on a contractor invoice

No federal law prescribes what an invoice must look like, but a complete invoice gets paid faster and holds up if there is a dispute. Include:

  • your business name, address and phone or e-mail, and your license number if your state requires it on your paperwork,
  • the client's name and billing address, and the job address if it is different,
  • a unique invoice number and the invoice date,
  • when the work was done, and a reference to the estimate or contract it belongs to,
  • each item of labor and materials with quantity, unit price and amount,
  • the subtotal, any sales tax, deposits already paid and the amount due,
  • the due date, payment terms and how to pay you.

Sales tax

Whether you charge sales tax on labor, on materials or on both depends on your state and sometimes on the type of job. Some states tax neither on real property improvements, others tax materials only. Check with your state's revenue department or your accountant, then pick “Add sales tax” in the template and enter your rate.

Payment terms, deposits and late fees

  • Terms: “Due on receipt”, “Net 15” or “Net 30” are the usual choices. A specific due date is clearer than any of them.
  • Deposits and progress payments: show what the client has already paid and subtract it, so the amount due is never in question. Some states limit the deposit a home improvement contractor can take.
  • Late fees: you can generally only charge them if the client agreed to them beforehand, in the estimate or contract, and some states limit the amount.
  • Lien rights: many states give contractors a mechanic's lien for unpaid work, with strict notice and filing deadlines. If a large invoice is going unpaid, check the deadlines in your state early.

Invoicing business clients

A business that pays you for services may ask you for a Form W-9 and report the payments to the IRS on Form 1099-NEC. For payments made in 2026 the reporting threshold is $2,000 in the year. It was $600 for earlier years. You report all your income either way, so keep your invoices numbered and filed.

Tips to get paid faster

  • Send the invoice the day the job is finished.
  • Describe the work the way the estimate did, so the client recognizes every line.
  • Offer an easy way to pay. In the Fixbill app you can add your own PayPal or Stripe payment link and the invoice gets a scan-to-pay QR code.
  • Follow up politely the day after the due date.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between an estimate and an invoice?

An estimate tells the client what the job should cost before you start. An invoice asks for payment for work you have done. The invoice should match the estimate plus any changes the client approved.

Do I need an invoice number?

No law requires one for most small businesses, but a unique number on every invoice makes bookkeeping, payment references and tax time much easier.
