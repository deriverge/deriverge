What to put on a contractor estimate

  • your business name, address and phone or e-mail, and your contractor license number if your state requires it on estimates and contracts,
  • the client's name and the job address,
  • an estimate number, the date and how long the price is valid,
  • the scope of work as line items with quantity, unit and unit price, with labor and materials shown separately,
  • the subtotal, sales tax if it applies, and the total,
  • the payment schedule: deposit, progress payments and final payment,
  • exclusions and assumptions, for example permits, disposal or hidden damage,
  • a signature line so the client can accept the estimate.

Estimate, quote or bid?

An estimate is your best calculation of what the job will cost, and the final price can change if the job changes. A quote or fixed bid is a firm price for a defined scope. Write on the document which one it is, and say how you will handle changes, for example with a written change order the client signs before extra work starts.

Sales tax, deposits and cancellation

Whether you charge sales tax on labor, on materials or on both depends on your state and sometimes on the type of job. Check with your state's revenue department or your accountant.

Some states limit the deposit a home improvement contractor can take. California, for example, caps the down payment on home improvement contracts at $1,000 or 10% of the contract price, whichever is less. The FTC Cooling-Off Rule can also give customers three days to cancel some sales made at their home. Check which rules apply where you work.

Tips to win more jobs

  • Send the estimate the same day as the site visit.
  • Add photos of the current state so the scope is clear.
  • Offer options, for example standard and premium materials, so the client chooses between your prices rather than between contractors.
  • Make it easy to say yes: a signature on the spot beats a printout on the fridge.

Questions people ask

Is an estimate legally binding?

An estimate is your best calculation, not a fixed price, unless you call it a quote or a fixed bid. Once the client signs it, the scope and terms on it become part of your agreement, so write clearly how changes are handled.

How long should an estimate be valid?

30 days is common. If material prices are moving quickly, choose a shorter period and print the date on the estimate.
